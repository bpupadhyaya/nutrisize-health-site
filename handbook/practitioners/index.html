Movement

Activity guidance and where clients stand

The guideline numbers, side by side

WHO (2020) for adults 18 to 64: 150 to 300 minutes a week of moderate aerobic activity, or 75 to 150 vigorous, or an equivalent mix, with additional benefit above those ranges. Muscle strengthening at moderate or greater intensity on 2 or more days a week for all major muscle groups (a strong recommendation, moderate certainty). Adults 65 and over: varied multicomponent activity with functional balance and strength on 3 or more days a week. The CDC states the adult minimum as 150 minutes moderate or 75 vigorous plus strengthening on at least 2 days.

Source: Bull FC, Al-Ansari SS, Biddle S, et al. World Health Organization 2020 guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour. Br J Sports Med 2020;54:1451-1462. doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2020-102955; Adult Activity: An Overview, CDC. https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html

What this does not show: WHO notes that benefits accrue below the recommended levels, so these are targets and not thresholds. Evidence was insufficient to set a numeric sedentary-time limit.
A free tool for this: The Nutrisize Health exercise library (5,404 exercises) can be filtered by equipment and difficulty, with how-to, common mistakes, breathing cues and contraindications on each. Free to browse, and it runs on your device.

Where clients stand: the strength gap

In 2020, 46.9% of US adults met the aerobic guideline, 31.0% the muscle-strengthening guideline, and 24.2% both. A pooled analysis across 32 countries found 17.12% of adults (95% CI 15.42 to 18.88) met both. Lifting was linked to 10 to 17% lower risk of death, heart disease, cancer and diabetes in 16 cohorts, with the largest gain at roughly 30 to 60 minutes a week.

Step targets and the cheapest first step

Across cohorts the mortality benefit of steps plateaued around 6,000 to 8,000 a day at age 60 and over, and 8,000 to 10,000 below 60 (15 cohorts, 47,471 adults). In US adults, 8,000 steps versus 4,000 was linked to hazard ratio 0.49 (0.44 to 0.55), and step intensity added nothing once steps were counted (HR 0.90, 0.65 to 1.27). A 2025 review put the inflection around 5,000 to 7,000 steps. For clients who do not exercise, short vigorous bursts in daily life (about 3 bouts of 1 to 2 minutes a day) were linked to 38 to 40% lower all-cause mortality in UK Biobank non-exercisers.

Energy needs: the 2023 DRI update

The 2023 National Academies energy report revised the estimated energy requirement equations mainly because overweight and obesity prevalence kept rising, and it widened the reference population to the general population. Adult physical activity level (PAL) categories: inactive 1.0 to under 1.53; low active 1.53 to under 1.68; active 1.68 to under 1.85; very active 1.85 to 2.50. The equations do not predict requirements above a PAL of 2.5. Updated estimates are generally higher for the least active and lower for the most active than the earlier values.

Source: Dietary Reference Intakes for Energy, National Academies Press, 2023. https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/26818/chapter/3

What this does not show: These are population-level estimating equations, not an individual's measured expenditure.

Nutrition

Food, nutrients and the benchmarks you will be quoted

Protein per meal: a practical distribution

A review suggests about 0.4 g of protein per kg of body weight in each of at least four meals, which reaches roughly 1.6 g/kg/day. If the 2.2 g/kg/day upper level is spread over four meals the maximum is 0.55 g/kg/meal. The review argues against a hard ceiling of 20 to 25 g per meal.

No single diet won the head-to-head trials

In DIETFITS (609 adults), healthy low-fat and healthy low-carbohydrate diets produced similar 12-month weight loss: 5.3 kg versus 6.0 kg, difference 0.7 kg (95% CI -0.2 to 1.6); neither genotype pattern nor insulin secretion predicted response. Adding time-restricted eating (8:00 to 16:00) to calorie restriction gave no extra weight loss at 12 months in 139 adults (net difference 1.8 kg, 95% CI -4.0 to 0.4). A Cochrane review of Mediterranean-style diets (30 trials, 12,461 people) found low-to-moderate certainty for clinical endpoints, with a modest systolic blood pressure fall of 2.99 mmHg.

Source: Gardner CD et al. JAMA 2018;319(7):667-679 (DIETFITS). doi.org/10.1001/jama.2018.0245; Liu D et al. N Engl J Med 2022;386(16):1495-1504. doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2114833; Mediterranean-style diet review. Cochrane Database Syst Rev 2019;3:CD009825. doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD009825.pub3

What this does not show: Trials with group counselling, 12-month weight outcomes; certainty was not graded in the abstracts for the two RCTs.

Hydration: total water, not only drinking water

The National Academies set an adequate intake for total water, which includes drinking water, other beverages and water in food: 3.7 L/day for men and 2.7 L/day for women aged 19 to 30.

Source: Dietary Reference Intakes for Water, Potassium, Sodium, Chloride, and Sulfate, National Academies Press, 2005. https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/10925/chapter/6

What this does not show: A 2005 population reference for adults 19 to 30. It is not a target for any one person, and needs rise with heat and activity.

Fish and mercury advice you can hand to clients

EPA and FDA advise lower-mercury choices. Pregnant or breastfeeding: 8 to 12 ounces a week. Children: 1 to 2 servings a week, from 1 ounce at ages 1 to 3 to 4 ounces at 11 and over. EPA also quotes the Dietary Guidelines reference of at least 8 ounces of seafood a week on 2,000 kcal.

Source: EPA-FDA Advice about Eating Fish and Shellfish, EPA, updated 1 October 2026. https://www.epa.gov/choose-fish-and-shellfish-wisely/epa-fda-advice-about-eating-fish-and-shellfish

What this does not show: Mercury-aware advice, not a disease-prevention claim.

Supplement use is common

In 2021 to 2023, 60.2% of US adults used a dietary supplement in the past 30 days (women 66.1%, men 53.9%; 75.9% at age 60 and over), and 38.7% used two or more.

Source: Dietary Supplement Use: United States, August 2021-August 2023. NCHS Data Brief No. 561, May 2026. https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/products/databriefs/db561.htm

What this does not show: The brief describes use. It does not assess need, dose or safety.

Sleep

Sleep as part of the plan

Sleep: what moves insomnia, what does not

A component network meta-analysis of 241 trials (31,452 participants) found that cognitive restructuring (OR for remission 1.68, 1.28 to 2.20), sleep restriction (1.49, 1.04 to 2.13), stimulus control (1.43, 1.00 to 2.05) and third-wave components (1.49, 1.10 to 2.03) were linked to remission, while sleep hygiene education (1.01, 0.77 to 1.32) and relaxation (0.81, 0.64 to 1.02) were not. In East Asian cohorts (322,721 adults), mortality followed a J-shaped curve with a 7-hour reference, and 10 hours or more was the strongest marker of risk.

Source: Furukawa Y et al. JAMA Psychiatry 2024. doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2023.5060; Sleep duration and mortality in East Asian cohorts. JAMA Netw Open 2021. doi.org/10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2021.22837

What this does not show: Sleep duration was self-reported in the cohorts. Abstract-level reading for both papers.

Longer sleep, fewer calories

Adults aged 21 to 40 with overweight and habitual sleep under 6.5 hours, randomised to extend sleep by about 1.2 hours a night, ate about 270 fewer calories a day over two weeks (95% CI -393 to -147).

Source: Tasali E, Wroblewski K, Kahn E, Kilkus J, Schoeller DA. JAMA Intern Med 2022;182:365-374. doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2021.8098

What this does not show: 80 adults, one centre, two weeks; no long-term weight effect shown.

Programmes and policy

What works at scale, and the regulatory backdrop

What lifestyle programmes achieve: trial versus real world

In the NIH Diabetes Prevention Program (3,234 participants), a lifestyle intervention aiming for 7% weight loss and 150 minutes of activity a week lowered the risk of type 2 diabetes by 58% versus placebo (71% for ages 60 and over), and metformin by 31%. In real-world Medicare delivery of the same programme (9,015 beneficiaries, 2018 to 2024), average weight loss was 4.9%, and 53% reached the 5% goal. At HRSA-funded health centers, documented weight assessment with nutrition and physical activity counselling for children 3 to 17 rose from 68.72% in 2021 to 75.22% in 2025.

Regulatory context: weight-management medicines

In the EU, tirzepatide (Mounjaro) is authorised as an adjunct to a reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity for weight management in adults with BMI of 30 or more, or 27 to under 30 with at least one weight-related comorbidity. EMA also recommended an oral semaglutide form 'together with diet and physical activity', and states that GLP-1 receptor agonists are not approved for, and should not be used for, cosmetic weight loss.

Source: Mounjaro EPAR page, EMA (page updated 8 October 2026). https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/mounjaro; First oral GLP-1 treatment for weight management, EMA, 22 May 2026. https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/news/first-oral-glp-1-treatment-weight-management; EU actions to tackle shortages of GLP-1 receptor agonists, EMA, 26 June 2024. https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/news/eu-actions-tackle-shortages-glp-1-receptor-agonists

What this does not show: Regulatory wording, not treatment advice. Medicines are prescribed by clinicians.
