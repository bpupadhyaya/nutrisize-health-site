Move, Fuel, Rest: what the numbers say

Seven findings from public health data and peer-reviewed research, each with its source and what it does not show.

Read it here, or download the PDF to keep.

Download the PDF

Move

One in four US adults meets both activity guidelines

24% met both aerobic and muscle-strengthening guidelines (US adults, 2020)

US adults meeting each guideline

National Health Interview Survey, 2020, adults 18 and over (self-reported)

Aerobic guideline46.9%
Muscle strengthening31.0%
Both24.2%

Source: Elgaddal N, Kramarow EA, Reuben C. Physical Activity Among Adults Aged 18 and Over: United States, 2020. NCHS Data Brief No. 443, CDC, 2022. cdc.gov/nchs/products/databriefs/db443.htm

What WHO recommends for adults: 150 to 300 minutes of moderate (or 75 to 150 minutes of vigorous) activity a week, plus muscle-strengthening on 2 or more days. Benefits also appear below these levels. Bull FC et al., Br J Sports Med 2020;54:1451-1462. DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2020-102955
47% lower
risk of death linked to 7,000 steps a day versus 2,000 (hazard ratio 0.53, 95% CI 0.46 to 0.60). Benefit levelled off around 5,000 to 7,000 steps.

Ding D et al., Lancet Public Health 2025;10:e668-e681. DOI 10.1016/S2468-2667(25)00164-1

10-17% lower
risk of death, heart disease, cancer and diabetes linked to muscle-strengthening, with the largest gain around 30 to 60 minutes a week.

Momma H et al., Br J Sports Med 2022;56:755-763. DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2021-105061

What this does not show: these are associations from surveys and cohort studies, not proof of cause. People who walk or lift more differ in other ways. The curve for lifting is J-shaped, so more is not shown to be better, and 30 to 60 minutes is not a proven optimum.

Fuel

Fewer than 1 in 8 adults eat enough fruit or vegetables

10.0% of US adults ate enough vegetables; 12.3% enough fruit (2019)

Share of US adults meeting daily recommendations

CDC Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System, 2019 (self-reported). Targets: 1.5 to 2 cups fruit and 2 to 3 cups vegetables daily.

Fruit12.3%
Vegetables10.0%

Source: Adults Meeting Fruit and Vegetable Intake Recommendations, United States, 2019. MMWR, CDC, January 2022. pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8735562

Ultra-processed foods supply 55% of calories

Share of calories from ultra-processed food, United States, August 2021 to August 2023 (NHANES, 24-hour recall)

All ages (1+)55.0%
Youth (1 to 18)61.9%
Adults (19+)53.0%

Source: Williams AM et al. Ultra-processed Food Consumption in Youth and Adults: United States, August 2021-August 2023. NCHS Data Brief No. 536, 2025. cdc.gov/nchs/products/databriefs/db536.htm

Protein per meal: about 0.4 g per kg, across at least four meals

A review suggests this reaches about 1.6 g/kg/day. The grams below are derived (0.4 × body weight), not measured values.

Body weight50 kg60 kg70 kg80 kg90 kg100 kg
Per meal (g)202428323640

Source: Schoenfeld BJ, Aragon AA. J Int Soc Sports Nutr 2018;15. DOI 10.1186/s12970-018-0215-1 (narrative review).

What this does not show: self-reported intake is not weighed intake, and a share of calories from ultra-processed food is not a measure of harm. The protein figure comes from a review of mechanistic and short studies, and the review argues against a hard "the body can only use 25 g" ceiling.

Rest

About 1.2 hours more sleep, about 270 fewer calories a day

-270 kcal a day after sleeping about 1.2 hours longer (randomised trial)

What the trial found

80 adults aged 21 to 40 with overweight who usually slept under 6.5 hours; two weeks; usual diet otherwise

+1.2 h
sleep a night (95% CI 1.0 to 1.4)
-270 kcal
energy intake per day (95% CI -393 to -147)

Source: Tasali E, Wroblewski K, Kahn E, Kilkus J, Schoeller DA. JAMA Intern Med 2022;182:365-374. DOI 10.1001/jamainternmed.2021.8098

What this does not show: one centre, two weeks, adults with overweight and short sleep only. It does not show a long-term effect on weight.
